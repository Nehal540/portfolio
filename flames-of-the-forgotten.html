01The intention

Flames of the Forgotten explores how a visitor might connect with a story through action. Its setting is a hidden forge,and its central interaction asks the visitor to create and sustain a fire through physical effort.

The deck frames the narrative around Malabar blacksmiths,identified as Kollan,in the early twentieth century. Within the project’s story,maintaining the craft involves working under the threat of discovery. The visitor is placed inside a small part of that tension: the fire must be kept alive,but excessive force can produce smoke that reveals the forge.

This is a designed interpretation of heritage. The setting is a narrative interpretation rather than an independently verified historical reconstruction.

02Choosing participation as the storytelling method

The visitor is not expected to be a blacksmith. They briefly enter a world where an apparently simple task,keeping a fire alive,requires attention and restraint.

The physical objects give that task a familiar logic. Striking initiates ignition. Pumping bellows sustains the fire. The screen makes the effects visible,while the intended sound and atmosphere give the actions a setting. The narrative appears through the consequences of how the visitor acts.

The design’s key tension is the relationship between effort and care. Stronger or faster input does not necessarily lead to a better outcome. The participant needs to notice the response and adjust their behaviour. This gives the interaction meaning beyond making an effect appear on a screen.

04Connecting objects,sensors and Unity

The technical slides specify a vibration sensor,HW-500,for the flint-and-steel interaction,and an ultrasonic sensor,HC-SR04,for the bellows. These components define the interaction approach; precise calibration and a repeatable setup remain important to document.

For the first interaction,a physical strike is interpreted as an ignition input. For the second,movement of the bellows provides input that the digital experience can translate into the behaviour of the fire. Unity is the visible response layer: environment,sparks,flame,smoke and narrative states connect the physical action to the story.

The interaction links what the person does,what changes on screen and how that response guides the next action. This relationship is the core of the physical–digital experience.

05Making rhythm consequential

The deck defines two endings. In one,gentle rhythmic pumping keeps the forge alive and protects the hidden workspace. In the other,pumping too hard and too fast creates thick smoke and leads to discovery within the narrative.

These endings make the same object support two different outcomes. The design depends on the visitor being able to read the response before the final consequence. If the relationship between pumping and smoke is unclear,the ending risks feeling arbitrary. If it is legible,the visitor has a reason to slow down,adjust and remain attentive.

The intended learning is embedded in the relation between action and response. The final text completes that relation by connecting it to the story the installation wants to tell.

07Building a multisensory experience

The deck lists tactile interaction through the objects,visual fire and sparks,sound including striking and crackling,burnt-ash scent,and a thermal effect using a hidden heater or hairdryer. These channels have different intended roles: touch gives the visitor something to do,visuals reveal change,sound reinforces activity,and scent or warmth would extend the atmosphere beyond the screen.

The demonstration video visibly documents a tabletop setup,physical props,an illuminated fire representation,a projected Unity scene and people engaging with the installation. It includes segments labelled as initial testing,setup/testing and reactions. Those are useful process artifacts,but they do not establish a formal study or measured learning outcomes. Scent,thermal implementation and exact audio behaviour should be labelled as documented intentions unless you can confirm and demonstrate their use.

08What the prototype demonstrates

The project demonstrates an approach to linking physical effort with digital narrative. The objects,virtual environment and ending logic are designed as parts of one experience. The strongest available evidence is the physical setup,the interaction documentation,the Unity work and the demonstration video.

The next distinction to make is between an interaction functioning and an experience communicating its meaning. Further evaluation is needed to understand whether visitors understood the historical framing,recognised the reason for the two endings,or independently learned how to regulate the bellows.

09What I would examine next

Next steps: Observe first-time visitors without explaining the sequence. Check whether they know where to begin,notice the effect of their actions and connect smoke with consequence. Ask them to describe the story in their own words. Document calibration and failure recovery so the installation can run repeatedly. Work with suitable historical sources or heritage practitioners to refine the narrative and its attribution.

Reflection

What this project brings forward

The story becomes meaningful when a visitor can read the effect of an action and choose how to continue. That relationship between movement,feedback and consequence is the centre of this work.
